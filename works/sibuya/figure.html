What Sibuya promised, and what is actually there

Row 161 of the Stirling triangle of the first kind. Both curves are exact rational numbers; the axis is logarithmic.

The blue curve is how far each entry of the row really sits above the geometric mean of its neighbours. The orange curve is the floor Sibuya wrote down in 1988 and could not prove. They touch at exactly one point, the first index, where the inequality is an identity. Everywhere else there is room, and the shaded band is how much.

The same picture, for rows of different length

The truth divided by the bound. One means they touch; above one means room to spare.

Every row behaves the same way: it starts flush against the bound and then opens up, and a longer row simply stays close for longer. That is what the theorem says — the bound holds for every length, with no threshold and no case that grows with the row. The tightest place is never in the middle and never at the far end — it is the very first index, on every row.
