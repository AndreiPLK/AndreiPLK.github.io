Checking is not knowing: the blades never touch the shore — proved

Our master-formula paper found the blades: extra kill-zones floating above the shore where theories of gravity die. Three million exact checks never saw one touch the water. This paper replaces checking with knowing: a proof, built from 1,047 exact-arithmetic certificates, that no blade of any level, at any coupling, anywhere along the infinite coast, ever cuts into the land of the living.

← Andrei Pluzhnik — all work

The beautiful part

Infinitely far out, the fleet of blades sails exactly parallel to the shore: the tangency discriminant of 6ρ² − (12+4√3)ρ + (8+4√3) vanishes identically at ρ* = 1+1/√3 — zero angle between fleet and shore. The theorem survives because every actual ship is confined strictly inside the wake (windows need ρ ≤ 4/3, asymptotic tip at √(5/3) ≈ 1.291). A naive proof must fail at the tangency; the containment lemma is what makes it possible.

How it was checked (and what the check caught)

The proof is a single re-runnable script: envelope branches for small couplings plus four deep-water lemmas over ℚ(√3). An independent adversarial review then audited every implication — and caught a real bug: the prover had silently skipped 740 cells while reporting success. Fixed; the reviewer's own battery (sharing no code with the prover) re-certified every cell including the formerly uncovered ones: no counterexample. That episode ships with the paper: an exit code proves what a script checked, not what it covered.

1,047 certificate cells · two independent provers, both exit 0 60,000-point stress test · margins bottom out near 2.15 dims exact tangency at ρ = 1+1/√3 AI involvement + the caught bug disclosed in full

Explain it to anyone

A house rule of this lab: every paper ships with a way to explain it to your family. Imagine a coastline where theories of gravity drown, and a fleet of blade-shaped danger zones floating above it. We had checked three million spots and the blades never touched the water. But checking is not knowing — maybe spot three million and one is different. This paper is the proof that it never happens: not anywhere, not for any blade, all the way to infinity — even though at infinity the fleet sails exactly parallel to the shore, touching it at a single unreachable point. And one more honest thing: our first version of the proof had a real bug — it skipped 740 cases while claiming victory. An independent reviewer caught it. We fixed it, re-proved everything twice, and put the story in the paper, because that is how science should work.
